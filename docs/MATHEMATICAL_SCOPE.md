# Mathematical Scope

## 1. The common object

Finite-dimensional vector spaces over $\mathbb{Q}$:

$$U, \qquad V, \qquad W.$$

A linear map

$$D : U \to V.$$

Interpretations such as regions, boundary corrections, tracks, sensor
comparisons, and local assignments are deliberately omitted.
Mathematically, $D$ says which presented differences may be generated by
permitted changes in an underlying state.

A presented residue $r \in V$, and a declared claim map $L : U \to W$.
The resulting instance:

$$\mathcal{I} = (U, V, W, D, r, L).$$

This is more general than ROC because it includes a claim map $L$. It is
more abstract than PCE because it excludes provenance formats, policies,
certificate schemas, and application-specific evidence.

## 2. The first obstruction: does the residue lift?

The problem is $Du = r$. Define the repair (realisation) fibre:

$$F_r = D^{-1}(r) = \{ u \in U : Du = r \}.$$

**Empty fibre.** $F_r = \varnothing$, equivalently $r \notin \operatorname{im} D$.
The obstruction is the quotient class

$$\omega_r = [r] \in \operatorname{coker} D = V / \operatorname{im} D.$$

This is the core situation ROC handles. A **dual separator** is a
functional $y \in V^{*}$ with $y \circ D = 0$ but $y(r) \neq 0$ — in
coordinates, $D^{\mathsf{T}} y = 0$, $\langle y, r \rangle \neq 0$. The
separator detects the nonzero class $[r]$.

**Nonempty fibre.** $F_r \neq \varnothing$: at least one reconciliation
exists. This does not yet mean the claimed value is exact — that is where
the second obstruction begins.

## 3. The second obstruction: does the claim descend?

Suppose $Du = r$ has a solution. All solutions have the form
$u_0 + k$, $k \in \ker D$. The claim values produced by the complete
repair fibre are:

$$L(F_r) = \{ L(u_0 + k) : k \in \ker D \} = L(u_0) + L(\ker D)$$

by linearity — an affine subspace of $W$. This equation is one of the
founding results of this repository: the set of possible claimed values
has an origin determined by one repair $u_0$, and an ambiguity space
determined by $L(\ker D)$.

Define the **ambiguity space**:

$$A_{D,L} = L(\ker D) \subseteq W.$$

$$A_{D,L} = 0 \iff \text{the claim is constant across the complete repair fibre.}$$

$$\text{claim exactness} \iff L(\ker D) = 0 \iff \ker D \subseteq \ker L.$$

This is the mathematical contribution PCE adds.

## 4. The two obstruction classes

**Lifting obstruction:** $\omega_r = [r] \in \operatorname{coker} D$.
Answers: does any underlying realisation exist?

**Descent obstruction.** Consider precomposition:

$$D_W^{*} : \operatorname{Hom}(V, W) \to \operatorname{Hom}(U, W), \qquad D_W^{*}(M) = M \circ D.$$

$L$ descends through $D$ exactly when $L \in \operatorname{im} D_W^{*}$.
Its descent obstruction:

$$\delta_L = [L] \in \operatorname{coker} D_W^{*}.$$

Answers: is the claim determined by the presented residue?

$$\boxed{\text{realisability defect} = [r] \in \operatorname{coker} D}$$

$$\boxed{\text{exactness defect} = [L] \in \operatorname{coker} D_W^{*}}$$

ROC studies the first defect. PCE introduces the second. This repository
studies their interaction.

### The descent exact sequence

The descent obstruction class $[L] \in \operatorname{coker} D_W^{*}$ is
canonically identified with a more concrete object. Let

$$\rho : \operatorname{Hom}(U, W) \to \operatorname{Hom}(\ker D, W), \qquad \rho(L) = L|_{\ker D}$$

be restriction to $\ker D$. In finite-dimensional vector spaces the
sequence

$$\operatorname{Hom}(V, W) \xrightarrow{D_W^{*}} \operatorname{Hom}(U, W) \xrightarrow{\rho} \operatorname{Hom}(\ker D, W) \longrightarrow 0$$

is exact: $\ker \rho = \operatorname{im} D_W^{*}$ (a map $L$ restricts to
zero on $\ker D$ exactly when it factors through $D$, by R2 in
[`THEOREM_LADDER.md`](THEOREM_LADDER.md)), and $\rho$ is surjective
because any linear map defined on $\ker D$ extends to $U$. Exactness
gives a canonical isomorphism

$$\operatorname{coker} D_W^{*} \cong \operatorname{Hom}(\ker D, W), \qquad [L] \longmapsto L|_{\ker D}.$$

This connects the quotient-based descent obstruction to the
gauge-direction formulation PCE uses. Three distinct levels of
information should not be conflated:

1. the **descent obstruction class** $[L] \in \operatorname{coker} D_W^{*}$;
2. the **restricted ambiguity map** $L|_{\ker D} : \ker D \to W$,
   canonically isomorphic to (1);
3. the **ambiguity subspace** $A_{D,L} = \operatorname{im}(L|_{\ker D}) \subseteq W$
   (Section 3), the image of (2) — it retains less information than (1)
   and (2), but is sufficient to decide exactness and to measure
   $\dim A_{D,L}$.

## 5. The three verdicts

`INADMISSIBLE` is left outside the linear theory — see
[`NON_CLAIMS.md`](NON_CLAIMS.md). The intrinsic linear theory has exactly
three verdicts.

**Obstructed.** $[r] \neq 0$ in $\operatorname{coker} D$, equivalently
$F_r = \varnothing$. No repair exists.

**Underdetermined.** $[r] = 0$ but $[L] \neq 0$ in $\operatorname{coker} D_W^{*}$,
equivalently $F_r \neq \varnothing$ and $L(\ker D) \neq 0$. Repairs exist,
but different repairs produce different claimed values.

**Exact.** $[r] = 0$ and $[L] = 0$, equivalently $F_r \neq \varnothing$
and $L(\ker D) = 0$. The claim is constant across the complete repair
fibre.

The verdict is determined by the **exactness profile**:

$$\mathfrak{E}(\mathcal{I}) = ([r], [L]).$$

### Profile versus verdict

The two axes are independent, so the exactness profile has four sectors:

| $[r]$ | $[L]$ | Sector |
|---|---|---|
| $0$ | $0$ | realisable and exact |
| $0$ | $\neq 0$ | realisable but underdetermined |
| $\neq 0$ | $0$ | obstructed, though the claim itself descends |
| $\neq 0$ | $\neq 0$ | obstructed and independently non-descending |

The operational trichotomy above is a *projection* of this four-sector
profile in which the lifting obstruction takes priority: both sectors
with $[r] \neq 0$ are reported as Obstructed, because there is no repair
fibre on which to issue an Exact or Underdetermined result:

$$\operatorname{Verdict}([r], [L]) = \begin{cases} \text{Obstructed}, & [r] \neq 0, \\ \text{Underdetermined}, & [r] = 0,\ [L] \neq 0, \\ \text{Exact}, & [r] = 0,\ [L] = 0. \end{cases}$$

Collapsing the two $[r] \neq 0$ sectors is legitimate for the operational
verdict, but the descent defect $[L]$ does not cease to exist merely
because the lifting obstruction is nonzero — it remains a latent property
of the declared claim map. If $r$ is later changed or repaired so that
$[r]$ vanishes, that latent descent status is exactly what determines
whether the result becomes Exact or Underdetermined. The complete profile
$([r], [L])$, not the collapsed verdict, is what R7 asserts is preserved.

## 6. The canonical value theorem

When both obstructions vanish, there exist $u \in U$ and $M : V \to W$
such that $Du = r$ and $M \circ D = L$. Then

$$M(r) = M(Du) = L(u).$$

Define $x = M(r) = L(u)$. This value does not depend on either choice,
even though neither $u$ nor $M$ is generally unique.

*Independence from the repair.* If $Du_1 = Du_2 = r$, then
$D(u_1 - u_2) = 0$, and since $L$ vanishes on $\ker D$,
$L(u_1) = L(u_2)$.

*Independence from the factorisation extension.* If $M_1 D = L = M_2 D$
and $r = Du$, then $M_1 r = M_1 D u = L u = M_2 D u = M_2 r$.

$$\boxed{\text{nonunique construction} + \text{invariance} = \text{canonical exact value}}$$

## 7. The intrinsic factorisation is not the matrix $M$

Write $\widetilde D : U \to \operatorname{im} D$ for $D$ with its
codomain restricted to its own image, $\widetilde D(u) = D(u)$. Then
$\widetilde D$ is surjective and $\ker \widetilde D = \ker D$ — it is the
actual quotient-like map through which the unique intrinsic descent
occurs.

The mathematically intrinsic factorisation is not $M : V \to W$ but

$$\overline{L} : \operatorname{im} D \to W,$$

uniquely determined by $\overline{L}(Du) = L(u)$, equivalently by
$L = \overline{L} \circ \widetilde{D}$. It exists exactly when
$\ker D \subseteq \ker L$. In finite dimensions $\overline{L}$ can be
extended to some $M : V \to W$, but the extension outside
$\operatorname{im} D$ is generally arbitrary.

This repository distinguishes:

1. the unique intrinsic descent $\overline{L} : \operatorname{im} D \to W$;
2. a nonunique ambient extension $M : V \to W$.

PCE uses $M$ because it is easy to represent as a rational matrix. $M$ is
a certificate representation of the unique map on $\operatorname{im} D$,
not itself necessarily unique.

## 8. The ambiguity object is richer than a binary verdict

Retain the entire ambiguity space $A_{D,L} = L(\ker D)$ rather than only
asking whether it is zero. $\dim A_{D,L}$ measures the number of
independent claim directions left unresolved by the observation. If
$W = \mathbb{Q}^p$, then $0 \le \dim A_{D,L} \le p$: one claim coordinate
may be exact while another remains underdetermined.

**Partial exactness.** Suppose $W = W_{\mathrm{exact}} \oplus W_{\mathrm{ambiguous}}$.
A projected claim $P \circ L : U \to W_{\mathrm{exact}}$ may vanish on
$\ker D$ even when the complete $L$ does not. The theory therefore
identifies the largest quotient of the claim space that is exactly
determined by the evidence. Let $\pi : W \to W / A_{D,L}$ be the
quotient map. Since $\pi L(\ker D) = 0$, the quotient claim

$$U \xrightarrow{L} W \xrightarrow{\pi} W / A_{D,L}$$

descends through $D$ — it is constant across each repair fibre. This
quotient is not merely one candidate with that property; it is
universal. For any linear map $q : W \to Q$ such that $qL$ descends
through $D$, $A_{D,L} \subseteq \ker q$, so $q$ factors uniquely through
$\pi$: there is a unique $\overline q : W / A_{D,L} \to Q$ with
$q = \overline q \circ \pi$. So $W / A_{D,L}$ is the universal maximal
quotient of $W$ on which the claim becomes exact, and every
underdetermined claim contains a maximally exact quotient.

## 9. The common diagram

$$\begin{array}{ccc} U & \xrightarrow{L} & W \\\\ \downarrow D & & \\\\ V & & \end{array} \qquad r \in V.$$

Two missing constructions:

**Lift.** Find $u$ with $Du = r$.

**Descent.** Find $M$ with $MD = L$.

If both exist, they meet in the canonical value $x = Lu = Mr$ — hence
**lift-descent calculus**.

Negative witnesses have a matching symmetry:

**Failed lift** — a separator $yD = 0$, $y(r) \neq 0$: $r$ lies outside
the image.

**Failed descent** — a gauge witness $Dk = 0$, $L(k) \neq 0$: $L$ fails
to vanish on the kernel.

The whole system is organised around $\operatorname{im} D$ and
$\ker D$.
